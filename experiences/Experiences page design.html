A private table on an empty bend of the river, set for you alone — lanterns, the chef's dinner, and a sky with no city in it.

The experience

A table where there should be nothing at all

There is a moment on a Nile journey when the day boats have moored and the river goes quiet, and a stretch of pale sand appears on an empty bend with no village, no road, no other soul. We set a table there — linen, lanterns, the chef's dinner carried ashore — and leave you to it.

It is the simplest of things and the rarest. Not a restaurant, not a "private area" of a busy deck, but an empty curve of one of the oldest rivers on earth, laid for dinner, yours alone until the lanterns are lowered and the only light left is the one that has been there for four thousand years.

How the evening unfolds

From the boat to the sand

Late afternoon

You sail out

As the heat lifts and the river empties, you cast off from the dahabiya. The crew has gone ahead; you arrive to find it already done.

Dusk

The sandbank

A table laid on bare sand, lanterns just lit, the river running gold and then grey at your feet. There is no one else, and nothing to hear but the water.

Evening

Dinner

The chef's dinner brought ashore course by course — Egyptian dishes done properly, with whatever you've asked for in advance. The crew serves, then withdraws.

After dark

The sky

When the lanterns are lowered, a sky with no city to dim it. The crew waits at a distance, out of sight and earshot. For an hour, the bend of the river is entirely yours.

Later

Back aboard

A short sail back to the dahabiya under the stars, and your cabin a few steps away. No drive, no gate, no return to a hotel — only the river, and sleep.

Before you picture it

The river chooses the spot

This belongs to a Nile journey under sail — it is not an outing you take from a hotel for the evening. And the exact sandbank is decided on the day: water level, season and wind all have a say, and in high water some bends simply aren't there. October to April is the season for it.

If conditions don't allow a true sandbank, we set the table on a private island or a quiet shore instead — still yours, still empty, still by the water. We will always tell you honestly which it will be, rather than promise sand and deliver a deck.
