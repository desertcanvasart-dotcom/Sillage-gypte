The journey

Short on days, not on Egypt

Not everyone has a fortnight. This is the short journey for those who don't — Cairo and Luxor, the two essentials, in six days. It leaves out Aswan, Abu Simbel and the Nile, and makes no pretence otherwise; what it keeps, it does properly, and never at a run.

Cairo gives you the whole sweep of the pyramid — from Saqqara's first step-pyramid to Giza — and the treasure of Tutankhamun in its new museum. Luxor gives you the temples and the royal tombs of Thebes, including, when it opens, the loveliest painted tomb in Egypt. Two cities, two short flights, and a great house of the Nile to come back to each evening.

Day by day

Six days

One way the days might run — yours to change.

Cairo & the pyramids · two days in Luxor · the medieval and Coptic city · home

Day 1Cairo, arrival+
Met inside the terminal, walked through immigration and the bags, and driven to the Four Seasons at First Residence. The rest of the day is left free — Cairo is a great deal to land into, and tomorrow is long.Stay · Four Seasons Cairo, First Residence
Day 2Giza, Saqqara & the Grand Egyptian Museum+
The whole story of the pyramid in a day. Saqqara first, and the Step Pyramid of Djoser — the oldest large stone building in the world. Then Giza: the Pyramids of Khufu, Khafre and Menkaure, the Sphinx and the granite Valley Temple. Lunch, and the Grand Egyptian Museum, with the complete treasure of Tutankhamun now under one roof. A full day; we pace it, but come rested.Stay · Four Seasons Cairo, First Residence
Day 3To Luxor, and the temples of Thebes+
A morning flight south to ancient Thebes. Karnak — two thousand years of building in one enclosure, with its hall of 134 columns — and Luxor Temple in the middle of the modern town, with the Avenue of Sphinxes that once ran between them, lately uncovered along its whole length. To the Winter Palace as the day cools.Stay · Mandarin Oriental Winter Palace, Luxor
Day 4The west bank, and the royal tombs+
The necropolis of Thebes — the finest day of painted tombs in Egypt. The Valley of the Kings, with Tutankhamun's tomb (small and nearly bare, but his) and the separate ticket for Seti I, the longest and finest in the valley. Hatshepsut's temple against its cliff, and the Colossi of Memnon. And, when the schedule allows, the Valley of the Queens and the tomb of Nefertari — the most beautiful painted tomb in the country, opened to only a few each day and closed when the authorities judge it needs rest. We secure it where we can, and never promise what they may not open.Stay · Mandarin Oriental Winter Palace, Luxor
Day 5Cairo, the medieval city+
A morning flight back to Cairo, and the Islamic city: the Citadel of Saladin and the alabaster Mosque of Muhammad Ali above the rooftops, then al-Muizz Street and the lanes of Khan el-Khalili as the day cools. Back to the Four Seasons for the last night.Stay · Four Seasons Cairo, First Residence
Day 6Coptic Cairo & departure+
As your flight allows, the oldest Christian quarter in the city: the Hanging Church, the church of St Sergius, and the Ben Ezra synagogue, all within a few quiet streets. Then you are driven to the airport and seen through to your gate.Depart · Cairo

Before you book

The short version, honestly drawn

Six days buys you two cities and no more. This journey gives you Cairo and Luxor — the pyramids, the museum, the great temples and the royal tombs — and deliberately leaves out Aswan, Abu Simbel and the Nile itself. If you have even three or four days more, they are worth adding, and we'll happily show you how; but if six is what you have, this is the most of Egypt you can see well without spending the trip in a hurry.

Two honest notes. Day 2 is a big one — Saqqara, Giza and the museum together — so arrive rested, and we'll pace it. And Nefertari's tomb, the loveliest in Egypt, opens to only a handful of visitors a day and closes whenever the authorities decide it needs rest; we hold a place when the schedule allows, and tell you plainly if it cannot be done on your dates.
