The journey

A river the convoys never see

Most Nile cruises move in convoy — a hundred and fifty cabins to a hull, the same temples at the same hour as a dozen other ships. The Private Nile moves at the pace of the wind and your curiosity. You sail a private dahabiya, the shallow-draught sailing boat that carried travellers up this river before the steamer existed, crewed by men who have lived on the Nile their whole lives.

It is the small scale that changes everything. A dahabiya can moor where the great ships cannot — at a sandbank with no name, off a village with no dock, below a temple before the day boats have left their berths. You reach Kom Ombo at dusk when it has emptied, Edfu at dawn before it fills. And an Egyptologist sails with you the whole way, so the temples are read, not merely seen.

Why it differs

Four things a convoy can't give you

II

Temples off the clock

Philae, Kom Ombo and Edfu timed to the hours the day boats aren't there — early, late, and sometimes alone.

III

Sandbanks, islands and village landings reserved for you — the nights are as much the journey as the days.

IV

An Egyptologist throughout

The same expert from embarkation to Karnak, who can actually read the walls, not a guide who meets you at the gate.

Day by day

Downstream

The Nile flows north — so the journey runs downstream, Aswan to Luxor, the current with you the whole way.

Day 1Aswan — embarkation+
Met at the airport and brought to your dahabiya. As the city lights come on, you cast off for an evening sail past Elephantine Island — the first hour on the water, and already the convoys are somewhere else.Moored off Aswan
Day 2Philae & the southern temples+
The Temple of Isis on its island, reached by private launch before the day boats arrive. Then back to the river and the long, quiet sail north, the Western Desert on one bank and the green strip of cultivation on the other.Moored below Gebel el-Silsila
Day 3Kom Ombo at dusk+
The double temple to Sobek and Horus sits right at the water's edge. We arrange it for the end of the day, when the cruise ships have gone and the stone turns gold — the way it was meant to be seen.Moored at a Nile sandbank
Day 4Edfu at first light+
The Temple of Horus — the most completely preserved temple in Egypt — entered at opening, while the colour is still cool and the courtyards are empty. The afternoon is yours: read on deck, swim off the boat, watch the bank slide past.Moored near Edfu
Day 5Esna & the river villages+
Through the Esna lock — the one stretch every boat must share — and into the gentler river beyond. We moor by villages most travellers only glimpse from a top deck, and you step ashore where the day boats never stop.Moored off a river village
Day 6Luxor — the West Bank+
Arrival at Thebes. We cross to the necropolis for the Valley of the Kings at opening and Hatshepsut's temple against its cliff — the tombs read properly, with time, before the heat and the coaches.Moored at Luxor
Day 7Karnak & departure+
Karnak in the clear light of early morning — the Hypostyle Hall before the day fills it. Then a private transfer to the airport, the river behind you and the journey complete.Disembark · Luxor
Before you book

This is a slow journey, on purpose

A dahabiya sails when the wind allows, which means the river sets part of the rhythm, not the timetable. Some afternoons are long and unhurried — deck chairs, a book, the bank passing at walking pace. If you want to tick every site at speed, a faster itinerary suits you better, and we will tell you so.

But if seven slow days on the water — temples read carefully, nights under a sky with no city in it — is the holiday you're after, there is nothing in Egypt quite like it. We would rather you arrive knowing exactly which one this is.
